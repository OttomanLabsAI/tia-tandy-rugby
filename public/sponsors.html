The Journey

I’m Tia Tandy, an 18-year-old hooker for Thurrock T-Birds in the Women’s Championship. I’ve been playing since I was eight — up through Kent county rugby and the Oxford Rugby Academy programme, with rugby league experience at London Broncos against top sides like Leeds Rhinos and Wigan Warriors.

Thurrock wanted to sign me a season early and went to the RFU for special dispensation — but front-row safety rules meant waiting until I turned 18. Now I’m the youngest player in the squad and the youngest front-row forward in the league, focused on one thing: developing, competing at the highest level possible, and inspiring more girls to pick up a rugby ball.

Alongside playing, I document the whole journey online — training sessions, matchdays, recovery, and the reality of balancing elite sport with everyday life. That’s what a partner buys into: long-term storytelling, in front of the right audience.

Why Women’s Rugby, Why Now

The Red Roses are world champions

England won the 2025 Rugby World Cup on home soil, and the women’s game is growing faster than ever — on the pitch and online.

The game has gone professional

England’s top-flight players now earn full-time salaries, and the standard below them rises every season — the Championship is where the next generation proves itself.

The Championship is watchable

Matches stream live online, so partners get real, visible exposure — not just a logo on a shirt nobody sees.

Where Your Support Goes

Even at Championship level, the women’s game is largely unpaid — clubs actively encourage players to secure sponsors to keep competing. Training in Thurrock two to three nights a week plus weekend fixtures, alongside full-time education, comes with real costs.
