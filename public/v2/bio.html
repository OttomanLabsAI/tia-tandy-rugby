Tia Tandy is an 18-year-old dual-code rugby player from Dartford, Kent, regarded as one of the region’s most exciting young front-row prospects. Competing at a high level in both rugby union and rugby league, Tia has built a reputation for her relentless work ethic, physicality, powerful ball carrying and fearless approach to the game.

Known on the pitch for her intensity, determination, trademark blue hair and unique “one sock up, one sock down” style, Tia is carving out a pathway through senior rugby with ambitions firmly set on reaching the international stage.

Senior Rugby

For the 2026 season, Tia has signed for Championship side Thurrock T-Birds and will make her senior debut in the Women’s Championship. Stepping into Championship rugby in her first adult season is a significant milestone — and a reflection of the confidence the club has in her ability and potential.

With Thurrock targeting promotion to Premiership rugby, Tia is expected to play an important role in the team’s ambitions this season. Her combination of youth, physical presence and experience across both codes makes her a valuable addition to the squad.

During the summer, Tia also represented London Broncos in rugby league. With the demands of Thurrock’s pre-season programme and their ambitions for promotion, prioritising her union season and reducing injury risk was the best decision for her development. Tia has also represented Brentwood Eels, further strengthening her experience in rugby league.

Grassroots Beginnings

Tia’s rugby journey began at just eight years old with Dartford Valley RFC, where she continues to represent her grassroots club. Starting in mixed rugby, she played in the U12s, regularly competing against boys — a challenge she embraced, and one that helped shape her physical and fearless style of play.

She later spent two seasons with Medway RFC at U14 and U15 level while continuing to play for Dartford Valley, balancing commitments between both clubs.

At 12, Tia began taking rugby to the next level, committing to an intense training schedule of five to six sessions per week. Alongside club training she worked independently and with her dad on swimming, hill sprints, fitness and ball skills, continuing to develop every aspect of her game.

Her dedication quickly translated into success. In her U15 season, Dartford Valley won their league while Medway finished third; Medway lifted the Kent Shield and Dartford Valley were runners-up in the Kent Cup. Despite being one of the youngest players in the squad, Tia made a significant impact — scoring 12 tries while playing mainly as a hooker, and showing her versatility by covering prop, flanker, number eight and even stepping into the backs at centre when required.

Elite Development

Driven by a desire to test herself and maximise her potential, Tia made a major commitment at 16, moving to train full-time at the prestigious Activate Oxford Rugby Academy. She spent two years developing her skills in a high-performance environment while continuing her academic studies.

Throughout this period she remained connected with her grassroots roots — returning home at weekends to represent Dartford Valley Beccs in mixed rugby, and taking on a leadership role as captain of Kent U18s.

Her journey through grassroots rugby, academy development and now senior Championship rugby reflects the commitment, discipline and ambition that have defined her career so far.

Off the Pitch & Media

Away from the pitch, Tia is passionate about sharing her journey and inspiring the next generation of female athletes.

Through her online video series, My Rugby Life, she documents the reality of pursuing elite rugby — from training sessions and match preparation to recovery, mindset and the challenges that come with competing at a high level. By showing the honest highs and lows of her journey, Tia hopes to encourage more young girls to get involved in rugby, believe in their ability and chase their ambitions.

With her sights set on continued growth, senior success and ultimately international honours, Tia Tandy is a young player with the determination and drive to make her mark on the future of women’s rugby.
